Cross-cutting observations

C1 dominates on tracking recurring expenses but is structurally limited on fraud (cadence) and silent on partner sharing. C2 owns partner sharing but at a real adoption cost — three of five participants explicitly said they wouldn't tag, and one experienced tagging as anxiety-inducing. C3 delivers on fraud and surprisingly also on tracking-via-anomaly, with sparse evidence to confirm. The emergent 'splitting bills with roommates' need is unmet by all three concepts and worth scoping for a future study — partner-sharing as designed assumes a romantic-partner relationship and misses roommate dynamics, which are mentioned by 40% of participants unprompted.
