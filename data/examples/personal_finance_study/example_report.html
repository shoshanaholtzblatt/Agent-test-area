03Methodological observations

Two patterns affect how the matrix above should be read.

P05 halo pattern

P05 rated <em>completely</em> across nearly every cell with vague, generic explanations ("sounds good", "this would help"). This is consistent with halo / acquiescence rather than considered judgment. Their ratings were down-weighted in every per-cell reconciliation. Treat P05's data as directional only — not as one of five equal data points.

Sparse coverage on C3

C3 was rated by only 3 participants. C3 was rated by only 3 of 5 participants because the study ran out of time. This is why every C3 verdict carries medium-or-lower confidence even when the signal is uniformly positive. A follow-up round bringing C3 to n=5 is the cheapest way to firm up the strongest single concept in the portfolio.

P02 contradiction on C3

P02 rated C3 <em>completely</em> on fraud while saying "I don't really trust automated alerts" in the same breath. Logged as a known contradiction worth probing, not as an undermining signal — the rating itself was for the concept's shape, the comment was about a category-level skepticism.

05Strategic implications

Four takeaways for the MVP roadmap conversation.

  • No concept is a complete solution. Building any one of these alone leaves at least two of three needs underserved. The MVP framing should be a set, not a single winner.
  • C3 + C2 is the most addressable combination. C3 owns fraud and surprises positively on tracking. C2 owns sharing. Together they cover all three needs. C1's distinct value (low-effort weekly summary) is real but not unique — C3 absorbs much of it.
  • "Automation with override" is the design principle the data is asking for. Across concepts, automation drove up-rating; manual control was claimed in theory and rejected in practice. The next iteration of C2 should test whether shared tagging can work with auto-suggestion as the default.
  • Roommate-style sharing is an unmet need worth a discovery study. 2 of 5 participants raised it unprompted; the concept set as designed doesn't see it.

01Study setup

Format: 60-minute moderated 1:1 sessions. Each session opened with a past-use story for each need, followed by stimulus-based concept presentation, followed by per-need ratings with explanations.

Sample: 5 participants, recruited for active personal-finance app use and at least one shared-spending context (partner or roommate). Mix of household structures.

Concepts tested: 3 stimulus-level concepts (early-stage; not click-throughs).

Needs tested: 3 functional outcomes from the team's job-map work.

02Verdict definitions

Addresses
Concept resolves the need as participants describe it, with consistent reasoning. ≥3/5 rate "completely" with substantive explanations.
Partial
Concept addresses some aspect of the need but a structural element of the stimulus prevents full resolution. Mixed ratings with consistent explanations.
Doesn't address
Concept does not address the need. ≥3/5 rate "not_at_all" with consistent reasoning.
Creates new problem
Concept makes the user's relationship to the need worse. Triggered by even one strong signal — surfaced at any confidence level.
Insufficient evidence
Below the 2-participant minimum for actionable evidence on the cell. Not an indictment of the concept; a signal that the cell is under-tested.

03Confidence definitions

High
Consistent signal across ≥4 participants with substantive, non-overlapping explanations. Halo-pattern responses excluded.
Medium
Clear majority signal but with one of: small n on the cell, mild contradiction, or one halo response that would otherwise tip the count.
Low
Single-participant signal, sparse coverage (n≤2), or significant contradiction. Reported but not actionable on its own.

04Reconciliation rules

Raw ratings are reconciled against participant explanations before a verdict is set. The skill applies the following adjustments:

  • Halo down-weighting: Participants whose ratings are uniformly positive across all cells with vague language have their ratings reduced to directional weight only. Detected once at study level rather than re-explained per cell.
  • Contradiction logging: Where rating and explanation conflict, the rating stands (it's the rated artifact), but the contradiction is flagged in reconciliation.
  • Creates-new-problem trigger: A single strong signal that the concept worsens the user's relationship to the need is sufficient to flag creates new problem, regardless of the majority verdict.
  • Sparse-coverage threshold: Cells rated by fewer than 2 participants are surfaced as insufficient evidence, not as a negative verdict.
