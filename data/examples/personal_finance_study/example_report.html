Research question

Which concepts most credibly serve customer needs for tracking recurring expenses, catching fraud, and sharing financial state with a partner?

Method

Concept testing — past-use stories + per-need usefulness ratings

How to use these results

Use for the MVP-scope conversation with product, design, and engineering leads. Each recommendation maps to a roadmap decision; treat sparse-coverage flags as a sequencing risk, not an indictment.

Key Insights

Insight 01

No single concept covers the full need set — the portfolio only works as a pair.

Building any one of these alone leaves at least two of three needs underserved — the MVP framing has to be a set, not a single winner.

Recommendation

Plan the roadmap as a concept pair (most likely Smart spend alerts + Manual transaction tagger) and design the joint experience, not just the individual concepts.

Insight 02

Smart spend alerts has the strongest fraud signal — but the evidence is sparse.

Smart spend alerts is the only concept that addresses fraud, and the same anomaly mechanism is a positive surprise on recurring-expense tracking — but the n is too thin to commit on alone.

Recommendation

Run a focused second round to bring Smart spend alerts to n=5 before locking it into the MVP.

Insight 03

Manual transaction tagger owns partner sharing — but at a real adoption cost.

Manual transaction tagger is the only concept that resolves shared categorization disputes — but tagging-as-mechanism is adoption-fragile and, for at least one participant, anxiety-inducing.

Recommendation

Iterate Manual transaction tagger toward auto-suggested defaults with manual override, then re-test with anxiety-prone users specifically.

Insight 04

Automation and low-effort concepts were preferred wherever they appeared.

Across concepts, the no-effort affordance is what participants rated up — and it shows up regardless of the surface (email digest, push alert). This is the dominant axis underneath the matrix.

Recommendation

Make auto-behavior the default in every concept; reserve manual control for override, not for setup.

Insight 05

Manual effort is claimed in theory and rejected in practice.

Where the concept asked for input (Manual transaction tagger), most participants said they'd rate it generously in theory but wouldn't follow through. The aspirational-vs-actual gap is structural.

Recommendation

Don't ship any concept whose value is gated on per-transaction manual work — fold the value into an auto-suggest layer with one-tap override.

Insight 09

Manual transaction tagger introduced money anxiety for one participant — small signal, big risk.

Supporting evidence

P03 · Manual transaction tagger × Catch fraud quickly"Tagging every transaction would stress me out, especially around money — I'd be anxious about getting it wrong, missing one, mis-tagging it."

So what

Recommendation

Recruit anxiety-prone participants explicitly for the Manual transaction tagger re-test; treat the auto-suggest iteration as a hypothesis that the anxiety dissolves once the per-transaction work goes away.

01Study setup

Format: 60-minute moderated 1:1 sessions. Each session opened with a past-use story for each need, followed by stimulus-based concept presentation, followed by per-need ratings with explanations.

Sample: 5 participants, recruited for active personal-finance app use and at least one shared-spending context (partner or roommate). Mix of household structures.

Concepts tested: 3 stimulus-level concepts (early-stage; not click-throughs).

Needs tested: 3 functional outcomes from the team's job-map work.

02Verdict definitions

Addresses
Concept resolves the need as participants describe it, with consistent reasoning. ≥3/5 rate "completely" with substantive explanations.
Partial
Concept addresses some aspect of the need but a structural element of the stimulus prevents full resolution. Mixed ratings with consistent explanations.
Doesn't address
Concept does not address the need. ≥3/5 rate "not_at_all" with consistent reasoning.
Creates new problem
Concept makes the user's relationship to the need worse. Triggered by even one strong signal — surfaced at any confidence level.
Insufficient evidence
Below the 2-participant minimum for actionable evidence on the cell. Not an indictment of the concept; a signal that the cell is under-tested.

03Confidence definitions

High
Consistent signal across ≥4 participants with substantive, non-overlapping explanations. Halo-pattern responses excluded.
Medium
Clear majority signal but with one of: small n on the cell, mild contradiction, or one halo response that would otherwise tip the count.
Low
Single-participant signal, sparse coverage (n≤2), or significant contradiction. Reported but not actionable on its own.

04Reconciliation rules

Raw ratings are reconciled against participant explanations before a verdict is set. The skill applies the following adjustments:

  • Halo down-weighting: Participants whose ratings are uniformly positive across all cells with vague language have their ratings reduced to directional weight only. Detected once at study level rather than re-explained per cell.
  • Contradiction logging: Where rating and explanation conflict, the rating stands (it's the rated artifact), but the contradiction is flagged in reconciliation.
  • Creates-new-problem trigger: A single strong signal that the concept worsens the user's relationship to the need is sufficient to flag creates new problem, regardless of the majority verdict.
  • Sparse-coverage threshold: Cells rated by fewer than 2 participants are surfaced as insufficient evidence, not as a negative verdict.

05Study observations

Patterns lifted once at study level rather than re-explained per cell. These shape how the matrix should be read.

P05 halo pattern

P05 rated completely across nearly every cell with vague, generic explanations ("sounds good", "this would help"). This is consistent with halo / acquiescence rather than considered judgment. Their ratings were down-weighted in every per-cell reconciliation. Treat P05's data as directional only — not as one of five equal data points.

Sparse coverage on C3

C3 was rated by only 3 participants. Smart spend alerts was rated by only 3 of 5 participants because the study ran out of time. This is why every Smart spend alerts verdict carries medium-or-lower confidence even when the signal is uniformly positive. A follow-up round bringing this concept to n=5 is the cheapest way to firm up the strongest single concept in the portfolio.

P02 contradiction on C3

P02 rated Smart spend alerts completely on fraud while saying "I don't really trust automated alerts" in the same breath. Logged as a known contradiction worth probing, not as an undermining signal — the rating itself was for the concept's shape, the comment was about a category-level skepticism.
