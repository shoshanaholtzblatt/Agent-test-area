Users can locate their account balance but consistently fail to complete fund transfers, with the multi-step transfer flow creating a 35% task success rate that signals high risk of abandonment and increased support demand.

Research question
Can users independently complete core banking tasks — checking a balance and transferring funds — without requiring support, using the current mobile app design?
Method
Moderated remote usability sessions with 15 participants; tasks scored using the Single Usability Metric (SUM) combining completion rate, satisfaction (5-point scale), and time-on-task at 90% confidence.
How to use
SUM scores below 60% indicate systemic usability problems. Prioritize Transfer Funds redesign before launch; use the Insights tab for ranked business-impact recommendations.

Insights

01

The multi-step transfer flow causes 6 of 15 users to fail or abandon the task entirely, and the remaining users take nearly 3× the expected time to complete it.

So what

Users who can't complete transfers independently call support or abandon the transaction — directly increasing support costs and creating lost-revenue events. With only 53% of transfers completing successfully in sessions, a production rollout would generate high inbound support volume and churn risk among users trying to pay bills or move money to savings.

Recommendation

Redesign the transfer flow to reduce steps and add inline validation with real-time field feedback. Present a confirmation screen that shows exactly what will happen (amount, from/to account, date) before users commit, and allow one-tap edits at that stage.

6 of 15 participants produced DF or IF outcomes on Transfer Funds. Mean time for successful completions was 142 s vs. a 50 s benchmark. Satisfaction mean 2.9/5.0 with several participants rating 1 or 2.

Task: Transfer Funds High
02

The Accounts tab label does not signal "balance" to users, causing 7 of 15 participants to navigate away first and increasing time-on-task before locating balance information.

So what

Users who can't immediately locate balance information lose trust in the app's navigation and are more likely to call support for basic inquiries. For the business, this represents unnecessary friction on the most frequent use case — balance lookup — and inflates session duration metrics that correlate with dissatisfaction.

Recommendation

Rename or annotate the Accounts tab to surface balance visibility at a glance (e.g., "Accounts & Balances") and consider showing the current balance as a persistent card on the home screen so users never need to navigate to find it.

7 of 15 participants did not navigate to the Accounts tab first. First-click accuracy was 8/15 (53%). 4 participants navigated to a different section before finding the correct path. Mean time on Find Balance was 68 s vs. 30 s benchmark.

Task: Find Balance High
03

Users who successfully complete transfers take an average of 142 seconds — nearly 3× the 50-second benchmark — indicating that even successful completions represent a poor experience that will erode satisfaction over time.

So what

High task time signals that even successful users are experiencing confusion or extra effort. Users who find transfers slow will downgrade to competitors with faster flows or use manual workarounds (e.g., calling the bank). For the business, this is a retention risk: friction in high-value tasks (moving money) correlates directly with intent to switch financial institutions.

Recommendation

Introduce a Quick Transfer shortcut on the home screen that pre-fills the most recent or saved recipient and amount, allowing frequent transfers to be completed in under 60 seconds without navigating through the full multi-step flow.

SUM time component for Transfer Funds: 0% (z = −3.35, well below benchmark). Mean completion time 142 s; benchmark was set at 50 s based on task spec. Even the fastest successful completions exceeded 80 s in session notes.

What's working well

The Transfers tab entry point is correctly identified by users who are familiar with banking terminology — participants who went directly to Transfers completed the task successfully.

4 of 4 direct-success participants navigated to Transfers as their first action. All 4 completed the flow in under 90 s.

Medium

Issues

The recipient selection step within the transfer flow is the primary point of failure — users do not understand how to add a new recipient or select an existing account, causing abandonment.

4 of the 6 failed participants stopped at the recipient selection step. Two explicitly said they did not know if they were setting up a new payee or using an existing account. Session notes record confusion about the "Add recipient" vs. "My accounts" options appearing on the same screen.

High

Many users conflate "Transfer" with "Pay Bills," leading them into the wrong flow before backtracking or failing entirely.

2 participants failed after entering Pay Bills and could not recover. 2 more entered Pay Bills but recovered via backtracking (counted as indirect success). The tab labels "Transfers" and "Pay Bills" are visually similar in size and proximity in the navigation.

High

Completion time for successful transfers (mean 142 s) far exceeds the 120-second benchmark, indicating the multi-step flow has too many friction points even for users who succeed.

The SUM time component scored 0% (z = −3.35). The minimum observed completion time was approximately 82 s in session notes. Even the four direct-success participants took between 82 and 110 s.

High

Recommendations

  1. Redesign recipient selection — Separate "Transfer between my accounts" (immediate, no recipient lookup) from "Transfer to someone else" (requires recipient setup) at the very first step of the flow, with clear labels and distinct visual treatment so users know which path applies to them. High
  2. Visually differentiate Transfers and Pay Bills — Use distinct icons, colors, or grouping to separate these two functions in the navigation tab bar, reducing mis-taps and wrong-flow entry. High
  3. Add a Quick Transfer shortcut on the Home screen — Surface a "Quick Transfer" card that pre-fills the most recently used or saved recipient and amount, enabling frequent transfers to complete in under 60 seconds without the full multi-step flow. Medium
  4. Add inline progress and step count to the transfer flow — Display a step indicator (e.g., "Step 2 of 4") so users can see how far they are from completing the transfer and do not feel lost or abandon mid-flow. Medium

Study Setup

Format
Moderated remote usability sessions conducted via video call with screen share
Participants
15 participants; mobile banking app users aged 22–58; recruited to represent primary customer segments
Tasks tested
2 tasks: Find Balance, Transfer Funds
Confidence level
90% (α = 0.10) — appropriate for usability research where precision is less critical than direction

SUM Dimension Definitions

Completion
Binary task success rate with Wilson score 90% confidence interval. Measures whether participants completed the task as defined without moderator assistance.
Satisfaction
Post-task satisfaction rating on a 5-point scale (1 = very difficult, 5 = very easy). Converted to a 0–100% score using a log-normal z-score transformation against a benchmark of 4.0/5.0. The displayed percentage reflects the log-normal distribution position, not a raw average.
Time
Time-on-task for successful completions only. Converted to a 0–100% score using a log-normal z-score transformation against a specified benchmark time. A score of 0% means all participants exceeded the benchmark; 100% would mean all participants completed in well under the benchmark.

Score Interpretation

The 80% dotted line in the Overview chart marks the industry baseline for good usability. Scores below 60% indicate systemic problems requiring redesign before launch.

≥ 80% · Good Meets or exceeds the industry baseline. Most users succeed with minimal friction. Suitable for launch; consider incremental improvements. 60–79% · Moderate Noticeable usability issues. A meaningful minority of users will struggle. Improve before scale; monitor post-launch. 40–59% · Poor Significant usability failures. A large proportion of users are affected. Redesign required before launch. < 40% · Critical Systemic failure. The majority of users cannot complete the task. Do not launch; fundamental design revision needed.

Confidence Level Definitions

High
Finding is supported by quantitative data (completion rates, time, satisfaction scores) and consistent qualitative observations across multiple participants. Unlikely to change with additional data.
Medium
Finding is supported by a mix of quantitative signals and qualitative observation, but sample size or variability limits certainty. May strengthen with additional sessions.
Low
Finding is based primarily on qualitative observation from a small number of participants or a single strong signal. Treat as a hypothesis to validate in follow-up research.
