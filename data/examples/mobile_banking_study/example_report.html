Prioritized UX recommendations

  • 1
    Fix or remove the Quick Actions transfer shortcut

    The shortcut routes to a different transfer screen than the Accounts flow and triggers re-authentication in some cases — causing abandonment and confusion across multiple participants. Align both paths to the same outcome or remove the shortcut until it is consistent.

  • 2
    Make the Accounts tab more visually prominent on the home screen

    Over half of participants missed the Accounts tab on first pass across both tasks — going to Settings, Help, Search, or Quick Actions instead. The tab label or visual weight may not signal "account information lives here." Consider a home screen balance summary that links directly through.

Qualitative themes

01Accounts tab discoverability

High confidence  ·  P06, P07, P08, P12, P14, P15 (6 participants)

Six participants across both tasks failed to notice the Accounts tab as a primary entry point on the home screen, defaulting instead to Settings, Help, Search, or Quick Actions. The consistent pattern of wrong-first navigation points to a discoverability problem on the home screen rather than confusion within the Accounts flow itself.

"I kept looking for some kind of overview but I couldn't work out where the account info would be — I tried settings but it wasn't there."— P07, ~5:45

02Quick Actions shortcut inconsistency

High confidence  ·  P02, P04, P06, P08, P09, P11, P12, P15 (8 participants)

Eight participants encountered the Quick Actions transfer shortcut. None were served correctly by it — the shortcut either routes to a different screen than the Accounts flow or triggers an unexpected re-authentication step. All participants who tried it had to recover through Accounts or gave up entirely.

"I tried the quick actions button but then it wanted me to sign in again, I just gave up."— P06, ~13:45
§03

Insight contradiction checks

  • Find Balance — 80% completion but 4 indirect successes. Completion overstates how intuitive the path is. Participants who recovered from wrong turns (IS) contribute to the completion rate without indicating the path was discoverable. The 53% first-click accuracy is a more reliable signal of the entry-point problem.
  • Transfer Funds — 6 direct successes despite critical-range SUM. The Accounts flow itself works; participants who started there completed the task correctly every time. The failures, abandonments, and low satisfaction trace back to the Quick Actions shortcut — not the core transfer experience.
  • Find Balance — P12 ease=1, satisfaction=5. Extreme frustration during navigation did not translate to low post-task satisfaction. Possibly the relief of finally finding the balance drove the score up, or P12 confused the scale direction. Rewatch the video before treating this as a confirmed finding.

01Study setup

Format: Moderated 1:1 usability sessions. Participants were given task scenarios and asked to think aloud while completing each task on the app prototype.

Sample: 15 participants per task (same participant pool across both tasks). Participants recruited for regular mobile banking app use.

Tasks tested: Find Balance, Transfer Funds.

Confidence level: 90% CI (alpha = 0.10). Satisfaction spec fixed at 4.0/5.0 (midpoint of "good").

Time spec: Find Balance — derived automatically (60.25s, 95th percentile of satisfied completers). Transfer Funds — manual (120s; no participant both completed the task and rated composite satisfaction ≥ 4.0, so no automatic derivation was possible).

02SUM dimension definitions

Completion
Wilson score interval applied to the binary completion rate. Reflects the probability that a given participant would complete the task, with uncertainty bounds that account for small sample sizes.
Satisfaction
Composite of three Likert ratings (ease, satisfaction, perception) expressed as a log-normal Z-score relative to the 4.0/5.0 "good" threshold. Scores below 4.0 produce satisfaction percentages below 50%.
Time
Log-normal Z-score of all participant times (including non-completers) relative to a data-derived benchmark — the 95th-percentile time among participants who both completed the task and rated it positively. Lower percentages mean the typical participant took much longer than the efficient benchmark.

03Confidence definitions

High
3+ participants with consistent evidence — quotes and path data point the same direction. Pattern is actionable.
Medium
2 participants with consistent evidence, or 3+ with mixed signals. Pattern is directional — validate before acting.
Low
1 participant, or quotes and data conflict. Reported for completeness — do not recommend action on Low confidence alone.
